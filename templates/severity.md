Every problem gets exactly one class: `blocker`, `major` or `cosmetic`.

- **blocker** — must not ship as it is: a wrong fact or number that people will see; a breach of law or of platform rules; a promise the business cannot keep; a broken step on the path to the goal (a link, a call-to-action word, a form); something the owner explicitly asked for is missing.
- **major** — will noticeably reduce the result or trust: a contradiction inside the work; a language error that a native speaker will notice; something unreadable on a phone; a part that will not work under the stated conditions; a fact without a source.
- **cosmetic** — taste and polish: correct as it is, but could be better.

The class follows the harm the problem does to the people who will see or use the work. It does not follow how easy the fix is, how confident you feel, or how many other problems there are. If you are torn between two classes, take the stricter one and say in your answer why. A class set too low is the costlier mistake: a problem filed as cosmetic is never checked again, while a separate check can still lower a class that was set too high.
